What is SHA-256?

SHA-256 (Secure Hash Algorithm 256) is a member of the SHA-2 family, standardized by NIST (FIPS 180-4). It maps any sequence of bytes to a deterministic, fixed-length 256-bit output through a series of modular arithmetic, logical functions, and compression operations.

Key Properties

Determinism Same input → always same digest.
Pre-image Resistance Computationally infeasible to reverse H(m) → m.
Collision Resistance Infeasible to find m₁ ≠ m₂ where H(m₁) = H(m₂).
Avalanche Effect 1-bit input change → ~50% of output bits change.
⚠️ SHA-256 Alone Is NOT Safe for Password Storage
SHA-256 can be computed at over 100,000,000,000 hashes per second on a GPU cluster. Without salting and key stretching (PBKDF2, bcrypt, Argon2), an attacker can precompute billions of hashes and crack most passwords in seconds.
